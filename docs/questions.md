# How questions are generated

Questions are written by `generateQuestions(scene, recipe, knobs)` in `src/scenes/generateQuestions.ts`. A round's 5 are picked by `selectQuestions` in `src/game/selectQuestions.ts`. This page describes both precisely enough to rebuild them.

**The one rule that matters most:** questions are written only from the facts recorded when the picture was made, never from the drawing. Those facts are each group's kind, count, colour(s), band and side, plus the picture's details (house colours and numbers, the time on the clock). The same facts are what get drawn, so every answer is right by construction. Laying the picture out again for another screen size moves boxes but never changes the facts (the scene checker verifies this).

See [question examples](questions-examples.md) for real pictures and every question generated from them (regenerated by `npm run docs:recipes`).

## 1. Where it fits

```
createStage(level)
  ├─ generateScene(...)            → SceneSpec (facts + boxes)
  ├─ generateQuestions(scene)      → the pool: every question this picture can answer
  │    └─ fewer than 12? → try the next seed (up to 10 times)
  └─ buildRound (useGameSession)
       └─ selectQuestions(pool)    → 5 questions, options shuffled
```

Each picture typically gives 25–45 questions; a round uses 5. **Same picture again** keeps the picture and picks 5 more from the same pool.

## 2. The questions written for each group

For every group in the scene (one per kind of thing), in this order:

### Count: "How many kites were there?"

- **Asked:** only for things that can appear more than once (`countable` not false and `maxCount` > 1). "How many mirrors?" would always be 1, a giveaway.
- **Answer:** the group's count.
- **Wrong options** (`countOptions`), from 1 up to max(6, count + 3), excluding the answer:
  - **Hard** (`closeCounts`): the three nearest numbers (4 vs 5), ties broken at random
  - **Easy and Medium:** three random numbers within 3 of the answer.

### Colour: "What colour was the kite?" / "What colour were the kites?"

- **Asked:** when the group has one colour (things with a fixed colour, like a crab, have none).
- **Wording:** the thing's `colourPrompt` if it has one, for things where only part is coloured: "What colour was the sail on the boat?", "…the lampshade?", "…the person's shirt?", "…the awning on the market stall?".
- **Wrong options:** the thing's other colours first, then any colour from `src/scenes/colours.ts`. Never red and orange in one question (`CLASHING_COLOURS`): testers mixed them up. That holds for every colour question, the combinations, house colours, true or false and "the same colour as".

### Mixed colours: "What colours were the fire hydrants?"

- **Asked:** on Hard, when copies were given different colours (`mixedColours`, fire hydrants only so far).
- **Answer:** the colours in alphabetical order, e.g. "Red and yellow" or "Orange, red and yellow".
- **Wrong options:** other combinations of the same number of colours first, then "All red"-style options and bigger combinations.

### Where: "Where was the umbrella?" → "On the sand, on the left"

- **Asked:** for every group except things spread across the picture (`spread` with 2 or more copies, e.g. street lamps along the pavement), which have no single side.
- **Answer:** `"<band label>, <side>"`. The side is the third of the width the group's centre is in. Placement keeps centres 5% clear of the lines between thirds, so it's never borderline.
- **Wrong options:** only the other sides of the bands this thing can go in ("In the sky" is never offered for a dog). A thing that only goes in one band gets three options: left, middle and right. Bands with no slot rows (the room's ceiling, the city's building fronts) are never offered.
- **Next to a landmark at its own depth:** furniture against the wall and tall things (lamps, the TV, a bookshelf, a plant; never a wide sofa) are placed next to each other, and things out on the floor next to other things on the floor ("Between the teddy bear and the ball"), never next to a bookshelf at the back: players compare things that are near each other. Things on a rug only next to things on it. Answers: "To the left of the table lamps", "To the right of the armchair", "Between the table lamps" (a pair standing apart), "Between the teddy bear and the ball" (two things, the left one first). Players found left / middle / right of a small screen hard to judge. Worked out from thirds, which never change when the picture is laid out again (a pair keeps each copy's third too); a landmark sharing the thing's third is left out. The wrong options are the false statements, about the same landmark first.
- **On a wall:** "Where was the window?" names what stood below each third: "On the wall on the right, above the table lamp" / "On the wall on the left, above the television" (one copy of a pair standing apart is "the table lamp"). A third with nothing against the wall is named with something that wasn't there, so the answer isn't the only one with a landmark, and one more wrong answer pairs the right third with the wrong thing. Without a landmark below the thing, the other styles apply.
- **No sides** (`sides: false`): most places never ask "where" by left / middle / right of the picture, which players found hard to judge on a phone; they use landmarks, places and bands instead, and "What was on the wall, in the middle?" is only asked with a landmark. Every place does this now (from 4.2.1).
- **Places, in a place that names them** (`recipe.places`: the living room, the park): loose things on the ground are "On the carpet" or "On the floor" (in the park "On the path" or "On the grass"), against the other bands they could be in and the place's other places ("On the sofa", "On top of the bookshelf"; "On the bench", "On the slide", "In the fountain"), where nothing loose is ever put. Only for things that could be in more than one: "On the pond" for a swan would be a giveaway. The landmark version is asked too, as another kind of "where" (`beside`); a round still asks only one. Such a room never falls back to left / middle / right: with no landmark, no "where" and no "What was on the wall, in the middle?".
- **On a rug** (the living room's carpet): things fully on it are "On the carpet" (`rug.label`), and that's a wrong option for things off it that could go on it. A group with some copies on and some off isn't asked. Whether a group is on the rug (`onRug`) is a fact like its side: laying the picture out again keeps it.

### What was here: "What was on the wall, on the right?" → "A mirror"

- **Asked:** only when exactly one kind of thing is in that band and side, and it isn't on a rug. Spread groups count as being in every third they have a copy in. Not asked for spread groups themselves.
- **A landmark on walls:** when one thing stands against the wall right below it (furniture or a tall lamp, the only one in that third; things out on the floor in front don't count), it's named: "What was on the wall on the right, above the armchair?". "On the right" alone was hard to place.
- **On the carpet:** "What was on the carpet?" when one or two kinds are fully on it, answered "A cat and a cushion" for two; wrong options mix one thing and two things (one of them swapped for something else), so the answer is never the odd one out.
- **Answer:** "A mirror" for one, "Mirrors" for several, using the thing's `article`.
- **Wrong options:**
  - only things that belong in the same band (never "Zookeepers" for the sky); with only two such things the question has three options, with fewer it isn't asked
  - always in the same form (singular or plural) as the answer
  - for a plural answer, only things that can appear in groups, never "Suns" or "Buses".

## 3. Questions about the whole picture

Asked when at least 3 kinds are in the picture and at least 3 of the recipe's kinds are not:

| Question | Answer | Wrong options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Which of these was in the picture?" | one present kind, picked at random | three kinds that are not in the picture |
| "Which of these was NOT in the picture?" | one absent kind, picked at random | three kinds that are in the picture |

They always say "in the picture", never "on the beach" or "in the street". Players read "on the beach" as "on the sand", so things in the sea or sky felt like trick answers.

### Questions that link things

These make you remember two things at once, or the whole picture, instead of one thing at a time. All are written from the same facts.

| Question | Asked when | Answer | Wrong options |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Compare:** "Were there more kites or more birds?" | two countable things (up to 2 pairs per picture) | the one with more, or **The same number** | always the three options in that order (`fixedOrder`) |
| **Most:** "Which was there the most of?" | 4+ countable things, one clearly the most | its plural, "Kites" | the other countable things in the picture |
| **Just one:** "Which was there just one of?" | exactly one countable thing was on its own, and 2+ had several | "A palm tree" | things with several copies, then countable things not in the picture. Never another single thing (the sun, the sofa), which would also be "just one". |
| **Above / below:** "What was in the sky, above the pumpkins?" | exactly one thing is in a higher (lower) band **in the same third**, and both it and the thing asked about are the only things in that third of their own band (in a deep band like the living room floor, an armchair at the back stands above crayons at the front, so "on the wall, above the crayons" would skip it); the question names that band, so "above the pumpkins" can't be read as the barn on the hill (plain "What was above the bench?" for the few bands whose names read oddly there: "Close by", "At the front", "Near the hills") | "Hot-air balloons" | things that can be in that band, from the picture or not in it, in the same singular or plural form; something that can't be there only to make up three choices, since it would give the answer away. Only the very next band up or down, never skipping one ("on the top shelf, above the milk" skipped an empty middle shelf, where players looked first). Never between two flat bands (the path and the grass, the sand and the sea at the beach): there the one lower down is nearer, not below, so "What was on the grass, below the dog?" made no sense. Bands are flat by default when they're ground, unless things stand flush on a line (shelves, roofs, the worktop); `flat` marks water that lies flat. Never into a band marked `inFront` (the supermarket floor stands in front of the shelves, so "What was on the floor, below the tins?" made no sense). |
| **In a band:** "Which of these was in the departure lounge?" | a band with two or more kinds | one of them | things that could be in that band but aren't in the picture at all, so exactly one choice is right |
| **Anything there?** "Was there anything on the carpet?" / "…on the pond?" / "…on the bottom shelf?" | a band (or the rug) holds one kind of thing, or nothing; not a band with a sign or door drawn in it (`keepClear`), not an empty sky (clouds and stars could count), not when something is hiding in the picture | "Yes, a teddy bear" or "No, nothing" | "No, nothing" is always one of the four, right when it's empty and wrong when it isn't, so it's never a giveaway; the rest are "Yes, a …" with things that could be there (on the rug, things allowed on it). Same subject as "What was on the pond?", so a round asks one |
| **Band:** "What was on the pond?" | only one kind of thing is in that whole band (not a rug's band, which has "What was on the carpet?") | "A swan" | things that can be in that band |
| **Two steps** (from Level 5): "Only one kind of thing was on the pond. What colour was it?" | one kind is the only thing in its band, all one colour, coloured as a whole (not a shirt or a sail), and nothing is hiding in the picture (a peeking crab could be "the thing" too) | "Yellow" | its other colours, then any colour. Same subject as "What colour were the boats?", so a round never asks both |
| **Altogether** (from Level 11): "How many kites and hot-air balloons were there altogether?" | two countable things adding up to 12 or fewer | the sum | the nearest numbers |
| **How many more** (from Level 27): "How many more crows than pumpkins were there?" | two countable things with different counts | the difference (1 or more) | the nearest numbers |
| **Sharing** (division, from Level 29): "If the fish were split into two equal groups, how many would be in each group?" | a countable thing whose count shares out exactly into 2 or 3 groups of 2 or more (4 or 6) | the share | the nearest numbers. The question never says how many there were: you remember it first |
| **Colour twins:** "What else was the same colour as the kites?" | exactly one other thing has the same colour | "The hot-air balloons" | things of other colours, then things not in the picture |
| **True or false:** "True or false? There were three kites." | up to 3 per picture, about counts, colours or things not in the picture | True or False (about half are false) | the other one, always True first |

Rules that keep them fair:

- **Above / below uses thirds, not exact positions.** Thirds never change when the picture is laid out again for another screen; exact positions can slide within a third. Spread groups and wide things (a sofa, a bus) reach into more than one third, so they're never asked about, and they count as "above" every third they reach.
- **Colour twins** are skipped when the colour also belongs to something with a fixed colour (green palm leaves, a red crab), a house, the barn, or one copy in a mixed-colour group: any of those could be argued as the answer. Things where only part is coloured are named by that part ("the sail on the boat", "the person's shirt"), taken from their `colourPrompt`.
- **False statements are clearly false:** a count one off (never below one), another of the thing's own colours, or a thing that isn't in the picture.
- Ideas we dropped: "Which way was the boat sailing?" and "What time of day was it?" were too hard to judge at a glance. "How many green things?" was dropped because the grass, palm leaves and trees are green too.

## 4. Questions about the picture's details (city)

Asked on Medium and Hard (`houseNumbers`), for each of the three houses:

| Question | Answer | Wrong options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "What number was on the yellow house?" | its number | the other houses' numbers first, then lookalikes: two neighbouring digits swapped (1564 → 1546), ±2, +10 |
| "What colour was house number 1564?" | its colour | the other houses' colours first, then other house colours |

House numbers rise left to right with uneven gaps, so remembering one doesn't give away the others.

### Any recipe's detail facts

A recipe can add facts about its backdrop in `details` (`DetailFact` in `src/scenes/types.ts`): an id, a type, a prompt, the answer and believable wrong answers. They're asked on the same levels as house numbers, with no other code. The farm uses one: "What colour was the barn?", answered from the barn colour chosen for that picture. New recipes should use facts rather than adding code here.

Some facts are about something hiding in the picture: from Level 20 the snowy mountain's danger-sign animal and the beach's crab, asked first in the round (`spotlight`) as "Where was the crab hiding?". When the hider is one of the recipe's own kinds (the crab), the recipe only hides it when none of that kind is in the picture, and lists it in `details.alsoSeen`, so it's never offered as "not in the picture" or as a wrong answer to "What was on the sand, on the left?".

Optional fields:

- `needs`: only asked when that kind of thing is in the picture
- `everyLevel`: asked from Level 1, not just from Level 8
- `spotlight`: asked first in a round when it can be
- `fallback`: more wrong answers when `wrong` runs short.

### What was happening: story questions (4.5.0, 4.6.0)

Things drawn differently per picture, about what was going on rather than reading a number or a sign. When the picture draws an extra one of something somewhere (a pupil at the piano, dogs pulling the sled, a rider on the snowmobile), that kind goes in `SceneDetails.uncounted`: "How many…?" and "What colour…?" aren't asked about it, since the extra one would make them arguable. They're facts with `family: 'story'`: asked from **Level 1** (reading clocks, numbers and signs waits for Level 3), at most one a round, and a round picks one whenever the picture has it, just after a spotlight question (they're the fun part of a picture, and rare). Two-choice ones (Yes / No, On / Off) are allowed, like true or false. Every answer really happens, "Nothing" and "None of them" included.

### Clocks (living room, city, park, kitchen)

When the picture has a clock (the living room's wall clock, the city's street clock or the park clock), it shows a time chosen for that picture (`details.values.clock`) and the game asks **"What time was on the clock?"** ("…on the street clock?", "…on the park clock?") on every level. Shared code in `src/scenes/clock.tsx` (`ClockFace`, `randomTime`, `timeLookalikes`), ready for a classroom or a train station (`randomDigitalTime` and `digitalLookalikes` for digital displays).

- **The time gets more exact as you level up** (`clockStep` in the level knobs):
  - whole hours up to Level 7 (3:00)
  - half past from Level 8 (3:30)
  - quarter past and quarter to from Level 18 (3:15, 3:45).

  A clock face never shows odd minutes: nobody reads 4:17 off a small clock at a glance.
- **Digital clocks** (the kitchen's microwave) follow the same steps below Hard, but on Hard show **any minute** (12:17), since digits read at a glance. Their wrong answers are digit slips: minutes swapped (7:45 → 7:54), an hour or ten minutes off, a 1 and a 7 mixed up.
- **Wrong answers tempt someone who only half looked:** the hands mirrored (3:00 → 9:00), the hands swapped (3:00 → 12:15), an hour off; then the minute hand a step off, or two hours off.
- **The face is drawn to read at a glance:** bold ticks at 12, 3, 6 and 9, a short thick hour hand and a long thin minute hand, 16% of the picture width (about 60 pt on a phone). It's never drawn mirrored (`noFlip`), even though other things flip for variety.
- It's a **spotlight** question: whenever the clock is in the picture, the time is asked (unless this picture already asked it).

## 5. Picking the 5 for a round

`selectQuestions(pool, { count: 5, preferredTypes, seenIds, previousIds })`:

1. **Not the last round's questions.** Questions from the round just played (Same picture again) are left out, as long as at least 5 others remain.
2. **Rank** each question, lower first, ties broken at random:
   - not yet asked about this picture, and a spotlight question (the clock): 0
   - not yet asked, a type the level prefers: 1; another type: 2
   - already asked about this picture: 3 or 4.

   | Practice | Preferred types |
   | --- | --- |
   | Easy | object, colour |
   | Medium | object, count, colour, position |
   | Hard | count, position, number |

   Levelled play moves through the same lists as you go up: see [Levels and scoring](levels-and-scoring.md#what-each-level-changes).

3. **One question per subject.** Every question has a `subject`: the thing's kind, or `house-<number>` for a house. A round never asks two questions about the same subject (e.g. not both the count and the colour of the kites).
4. **The variety rule.** Every question has a kind (`family`). A round has at most **one** of each of count, colour, where, what-here, in/not in the picture, compare, most/just one, above/below and colour twins; at most two true-or-false or detail questions; and at most **two of any one type** (so "how many", "more or fewer" and "the most of" don't fill a round together). Five questions therefore always span at least three kinds.
5. If the rules leave fewer than 5, a second pass relaxes the variety rule, then a last pass fills any gap.
6. **Shuffle each question's options** (`toRoundQuestion`) and point the answer at its new position. True or false and compare questions keep their order (`fixedOrder`).

## 6. In the quiz and afterwards

- The numbered dots above the question turn green for a right answer and red for a wrong one.
- A right answer flashes green and moves on. A wrong one shakes, shows the right answer and waits for **Next**. Questions can be skipped and answered later.
- The result screen shows every question with all its options (four, or two for true or false):
  - the right answer in green with ✓
  - a wrong pick in red, struck through, labelled "your answer"
  - the other options dimmed.

  Tapping the picture shows it full size.
- Each round is saved (`RoundResult` in `src/types/stats.ts`) with the score per question type (`byType`). The progress screen shows accuracy per type and its trend (`src/stats/summarize.ts`).

## 7. Determinism, ids and types

- **Same picture, same questions.** Wording and wrong options come from `createRng(seed ^ 0x9e3779b9)`. Only the round's selection and the option order use `Math.random`.
- **Question id:** `<scene id>-<template>-<kind>`. Templates are `count`, `colour`, `colours`, `where`, `what-here`, `seen`, `missing`, `compare`, `most`, `above`, `below`, `twins` and `true-false`. Houses use `<scene id>-house-number-<n>` and `<scene id>-house-colour-<n>`; detail facts `<scene id>-fact-<id>`.
- **Options:** two to four. Every wrong option must be believable: things that could really be in that band, places the thing could really be. When there aren't three believable ones, a question has three options; with fewer than three in all it isn't asked (true or false has its own two; compare always has three).
- **Question types** (`QuestionType` in `src/types/game.ts`), shown on the quiz chip and tracked on the progress screen:
  - `object`: what was here, in / NOT in the picture
  - `count`: also compare, most, just one
  - `colour`: colours, mixed colours, house colour, colour twins
  - `position`: where, above / below
  - `number`: house numbers, the clock
  - true or false takes the type of what it's about.

## Adding a question type

1. Add the type to `QuestionType`, plus its labels in `src/components/QuizStage.tsx` and `src/app/stats.tsx`, and add it to `QUESTION_TYPES` in `src/stats/summarize.ts`.
2. Write it in `generateQuestions` from scene facts only, with a `subject` so a round doesn't double up and a `family` for the variety rule (add a new `QuestionFamily` if it's a new kind, and its cap in `selectQuestions`).
3. Decide which levels ask it (a knob in `src/game/difficulty.ts`) and whether any level prefers it.
4. Run `npm run docs:recipes` and read the examples. Then play it with [My places](testing.md#on-a-phone-expo-go) set to the place you're checking, reading every question against the picture.
